The ontology: your wiki as queryable, governed linked data

Wikantik doesn't just store prose — it maintains a formal model of what every page is about. Pages, clusters, and tags are projected into a wikantik: RDF/OWL ontology with stable, dereferenceable IRIs and public mappings to schema.org, SKOS, Dublin Core, and PROV-O. The wiki becomes standards-based linked data you can query with SPARQL — and the model is derived from page frontmatter, so it stays in step with the content.

What the ontology is

The ontology is one model with two layers, projected from PostgreSQL into a Jena TDB2 triple store with RDFS inference:

  • The vocabulary (T-Box). The closed definitions everything conforms to — a Page class with five content classes (Hub, Article, Reference, Runbook, DesignDoc) and a SKOS concept scheme. Each maps out to public vocabularies (schema.org, SKOS, Dublin Core, PROV-O), so the model speaks the web's language, not a private one.
  • Page and concept instances. Every page is a typed concept — its content class, IRI, title and summary — and its cluster and tags become SKOS concepts it links to. This is the topical hierarchy, authored entirely from page frontmatter.

The model covers pages and concepts only: there are no extracted entities and no relationship predicates. The model stays in sync automatically: a page's graph is re-projected when it's saved or renamed, and a nightly job reconciles the whole store as a backstop — so the RDF is never stale for long.

Use case: ask the wiki structured questions, don't grep the text

Keyword search maps terms to documents. An ontology maps concepts to one another — so you can ask structured questions a text index can't answer. A public, read-only SPARQL endpoint at /sparql takes SELECT / ASK / CONSTRUCT queries (updates are rejected, with a result cap and timeout):

  • "Which pages are in this cluster?" — traverse skos:broader and the page-to-concept links.
  • "Which runbooks and design docs carry a given tag?" — filter on content class and tag concepts.
  • "Give me every runbook verified in the last quarter, as JSON-LD." — filter on the projected metadata.

Pages and concepts dereference at /id/page/{id} and /id/concept/{id} as JSON-LD or Turtle (e.g. a page's /id/page/{canonical_id}), and the whole model is downloadable as a live Turtle dump or N-Triples. A public/restricted ACL split means content that isn't anonymously viewable never materialises in the public dataset — so the linked-data surface is safe to expose. Any RDF tool — a triple store, a SPARQL dashboard, a reasoner — can consume the wiki directly.

Use case: a model that can't drift from the content

Most "AI wiki" tools bolt a chatbot onto a document store. Wikantik's model is derived from the pages themselves, so there is no second copy to curate and nothing to fall out of step:

  • Frontmatter is the source. Content class, cluster and tags come from each page's structured frontmatter, which the editor and the MCP tools validate against the same server-authoritative schema.
  • Event-driven sync. Saves, renames and deletes re-project just the affected page's graph and its concept graphs; a nightly rebuild also prunes anything stale.
  • Public/restricted split. Only anonymously viewable pages are ever materialised, so restricted content cannot be reached through SPARQL.
  • A drift burn-down dashboard. A corpus-wide sweep counts every remaining metadata warning, with trends and per-item drill-downs — so inconsistency is measurable and can be driven to zero.

SEO that can't drift

A page's public structured data and its internal model are two faces of one truth: the schema.org @type and sameAs emitted for search engines are re-sourced from the ontology (a hub becomes a CollectionPage, a runbook a HowTo, a design doc a TechArticle). You can't have rich, correct structured data that silently disagrees with what the wiki actually models — because they come from the same place.

The ontology and the Page Graph

The ontology is the formal, standards-based view of what pages are and which clusters and tags they belong to. It is not the Page Graph, the separate graph of real author-written wikilinks used for navigation, backlinks and neighbor lookups. See the Page Graph page for that side.

Frequently asked questions

What does the ontology contain?

The ontology is a formal RDF/OWL model of the wiki's pages and concepts. Each page is a typed concept (Page, plus Hub, Article, Reference, Runbook, or DesignDoc), and each cluster and tag is a SKOS concept. It is stored in Apache Jena TDB2 with RDFS subclass inference and is queryable over SPARQL. It models pages and concepts only — there are no extracted entities or relationship predicates.

Can I query the wiki with SPARQL?

Yes — a public, read-only endpoint at /sparql accepts SELECT, ASK, and CONSTRUCT (with a result cap and timeout). Pages and concepts dereference at /id/page/{id} and /id/concept/{id} as JSON-LD or Turtle, and full dumps live at /export/ontology.ttl and /export/graph.nt. A public/restricted ACL split keeps non-public content out of the dataset.

How does the ontology stay current?

A page's graph is re-projected when it is saved or renamed (and removed on a true delete), and a nightly rebuild reconciles the whole store as a backstop. The projection is derived from page frontmatter, so the ontology cannot drift from the content. The schema.org type in each page's public structured data is derived from the same ontology mapping.
